EXACT CLAIM

Let d=3 with the Bell convention of Eq. (1), B0=Phi_{0,0}, B1=Phi_{1,0}, B2=Phi_{0,1}, and rho(r)=sum_i r_i|Bi><Bi|. Write L=log2(3), u(z)=2sqrt(z(1-z)), and f(z)=H((1-u(z))/3,(2+u(z))/6,(2+u(z))/6). There is a unique a in (1/2,1) satisfying (1-a)f'(a)+f(a)-L=0; moreover 9/10<a<16/17. Define C=(L-f(a))/(1-a). For every probability triple r with m=max_i r_i>=a, E_F(rho(r))=L-C(1-m). An optimal ensemble has at most nineteen states: choose the unique dominant index i; for each j!=i take the nine images of sqrt(a)Bi+exp(i*pi/3)sqrt(1-a)Bj under conjugate(W) tensor W, W=X^s Z^t, s,t in Z3, each with weight r_j/[9(1-a)]; add Bi with weight (r_i-a)/(1-a). Zero-weight entries may be omitted. This is a computer-assisted theorem for three two-dimensional near-vertex triangles, not a solution for arbitrary Bell-diagonal states.

FULL FROZEN PROOF

All unmarked logarithms are natural; H denotes entropy in bits.

1. Phase minimization. For a unit vector sum_i c_i Bi, put r_i=|c_i|² and A=(c0 I+c1 X+c2 Z)/sqrt(3). Its coefficient matrix is A transposed, so its Schmidt eigenvalues are those of AA*. Direct expansion gives Tr(AA*)=1, Tr((AA*)²)=(2-sum_i r_i²)/3, and det(AA*)=|c0³+c1³+c2³|²/27. Thus its eigenvalues solve lambda³-lambda²+e lambda-D=0, with e=(1+sum_i r_i²)/6.

Implicit differentiation, partial fractions, and the integral representation of the logarithm give
H_D=(1/ln2) integral_0^infinity dt/P(t),  H_e=(1/ln2) integral_0^infinity t dt/P(t),
where P(t)=t³+t²+et+D=product_i(t+lambda_i). Initially these identities hold at distinct positive eigenvalues; the integrals extend them continuously through positive collisions. Consequently H increases with D at fixed e. The possible magnitudes of c0³+c1³+c2³ form an interval, whose minimum follows from the polygon inequality. Therefore the phase minimum epsilon(r) is the entropy of the cubic roots with
D=D_min=[max(0,2max_i r_i^(3/2)-sum_i r_i^(3/2))]²/27.
Zero eigenvalues are included by continuity. In particular epsilon is invariant under all coordinate permutations, without requiring a physical permutation symmetry.

For positive spectra, H_e/H_D<=1/3. Indeed, with c=1/3, the derivative of P(t)/(t+c)³ has numerator (2/3-2e)t+e/3-3D. Both coefficients are nonnegative because e<=1/3 and e/D=sum_i 1/lambda_i>=9. Hence h(t)=(t+c)³/P(t) is nonincreasing. Weighting the density proportional to (t+c)^(-3) by h cannot increase its mean: integrate (t-s)(h(t)-h(s))<=0 against the product density. The original mean is c, and the new mean is H_e/H_D.

2. High strip. Write r=(x,(1-x+y)/2,(1-x-y)/2), 13/16<=x<1, 0<=y<=1-x. Set s=1-x, S=sqrt(r1)+sqrt(r2), and t=x^(3/2)-r1^(3/2)-r2^(3/2). Then
 t/S=x^(3/2)/S-s+sqrt(r1 r2)>=x^(3/2)/sqrt(2s)-s>1.
For the last inequality, x³-2(1-x)(2-x)² is increasing on this interval and equals 31/4096>0 at 13/16. Thus D_min=t²/27>0. For 0<y<s,
 e_y=y/6, D_y=-ty/(18S), epsilon_y=(y H_D/18)(3H_e/H_D-t/S)<0.
The integral derivatives handle positive eigenvalue collisions; continuity handles endpoints. Hence epsilon(r)>=epsilon(x,1-x,0)=f(x). The edge spectrum is ((1-u)/3,(2+u)/6,(2+u)/6), as follows by diagonalizing the edge matrix or substituting its invariants. The assertion extends to x=1.

3. Edge contact. Symmetry gives f(z)=f(1-z). For 0<z<1/2 put ell(u)=ln((2+u)/(2(1-u))). Direct differentiation yields
 f_u=-ell/(3ln2), u'=2(1-2z)/u, u''=-4/u³,
 f''(z)=4B(u)/(3ln2 u³), B(u)=ell(u)-3u(1+u)/(2+u),
 B'(u)=3u(u²+3u-1)/((1-u)(2+u)²).
Thus B decreases from zero and then increases to infinity, crossing zero once. Since u increases on this interval, f'' changes sign once, from negative to positive. Set g(z)=zf'(z)-f(z)+L. Then g'=zf'', g(0+)=0, and g(1/2-)=L-1>0; the endpoint derivatives are f'(0+)=-1/ln2 and f'(1/2-)=0. It follows that g has one zero z0, negative before and positive afterwards.

For exact brackets let A(u)=(1-u)ln(1-u)+(2+u)ln(1+u/2). The identity 3ln2 g(z)=A(u)-[2z(1-2z)/u]ell(u) gives
3ln2 g(1/17)=(3/4)ln(83349/83521)<0,
3ln2 g(1/10)=(1/3)ln(250994068/250000000)>0.
Thus a=1-z0 satisfies the stated equation and bounds. Moreover ((L-f(z))/z)'=-g(z)/z², so this quotient has its unique maximum on (0,1/2] at z0. For z>1/2 its value is smaller than at 1-z, by positivity and symmetry of its numerator. Therefore C is its global maximum and
 f(w)>=U(w):=L-C(1-w), 0<=w<=1,
with equality at a and 1. In particular C>0.

4. Certified remaining wedge. The frozen standalone certificate proves, with interval arithmetic,
 epsilon(x,(1-x+y)/2,(1-x-y)/2)-[L-(1517/1000)(1-x)]>1/10000000
on K={1/3<=x<=13/16, 0<=y<=min(1-x,3x-1)}. It also proves
10[L-f(9/10)]-1517/1000>1/1250.
Section 3 implies C>=10[L-f(9/10)]>1517/1000. Consequently the certified plane lies above U(x).

For completeness, the computational enclosure and coverage argument is as follows. The certificate uses mpmath interval arithmetic at 35 decimal digits. Float endpoint conversions are enlarged by one outward nextafter step, preserving exact zero. On each rectangle it encloses r, e and D_min; on K the largest coordinate is x, so D_min=(max(0,x^(3/2)-r1^(3/2)-r2^(3/2)))²/27. Intersections with known coordinate and eigenvalue ranges preserve all feasible values. Cubic roots are enclosed using
 v=1/9-e/3, z=(1/27-e/6+D/2)/v^(3/2),
 alpha=atan2(sqrt(1-z²),z)/3,
 lambda_k=1/3+2sqrt(v)cos(alpha-2pi k/3).
The formula is used only with certified v>0; intersecting z with [-1,1] is justified by feasibility. Each eigenvalue entropy interval uses both endpoints and, if included, the sole stationary point 1/exp(1) of -t log2(t). Summing gives an entropy enclosure without assuming eigenvalue order.

A second lower bound is H>=-log2 Tr((AA*)²), by Jensen's inequality with weights lambda_i. The checker takes the best available certified bound. For rectangles wholly contained in K it can also use a center-based mean-value bound. It certifies -1<z<1, positive eigenvalues and nonzero implicit-root denominators before using gradients
 lambda'=(D'-e'lambda)/(3lambda²-2lambda+e).
Here D=(max(0,t))²/27 is continuously differentiable even at t=0. The gap's x-gradient subtracts 1517/1000. Every center-to-point segment stays in the rectangle. Failed root or derivative tests only discard optional bounds.

The initial rectangle is [nextafter(1/3,-infinity),13/16] times [0,1/2], which contains K. Splits create two closed rectangles covering their parent. Exact Fraction comparisons of binary endpoints prune [a,c] times [u,v] only if u>1-a or u>3c-1; either condition excludes intersection with K. The optional derivative bound requires v<=1-c and v<=3a-1, ensuring full feasibility. Only boxes with strictly positive certified lower bounds are accepted. The queue empties after 8609 splits and 8417 accepted boxes. The minimum accepted lower bound is compared, using exact rationals, to 1/10000000. This proves coverage including boundaries, not merely positivity at sampled points. The slope margin is similarly compared to 1/1250. The exact rational signs used in Sections 2 and 3 are also asserted and printed. The accompanying stdout records these assertions. The interval implementation is a computational trust assumption, not a proof-assistant verification.

5. Unrestricted lower bound. Sort any probability triple q as (x,(1-x+y)/2,(1-x-y)/2), with x=max_i q_i and y>=0. Then x>=1/3 and y<=min(1-x,3x-1). Section 4 covers x<=13/16; Sections 2 and 3 cover x>=13/16. Permutation invariance therefore gives epsilon(q)>=U(max_i q_i). Since U is increasing, epsilon(q)>=U(q_i) for every fixed label i, even when that coordinate is not largest.

Every positive-weight pure component of a decomposition of rho(r) lies in its three-Bell support: projection onto the orthogonal complement gives a sum of nonnegative squared norms equal to zero. Its entropy is at least epsilon(q)>=U(q_i), where q consists of its squared Bell coefficients. Averaging and using sum_component weight*q_i=r_i yields E_F(rho(r))>=U(r_i) for every i. No restriction on component probabilities remains.

6. Attainment. Suppose r_i>=a. For j!=i let v_ij=sqrt(a)Bi+exp(i*pi/3)sqrt(1-a)Bj. The two nonzero coefficient cubes have opposite phases, so Section 1 gives entropy f(a) for every ordered pair. For W=X^s Z^t the local unitary conjugate(W) tensor W acts on Bell labels by V -> WVW*. The characters on B0,B1,B2 are respectively 1, omega^t, omega^(-s). Thus averaging the nine images of v_ij removes its cross term and yields a|Bi><Bi|+(1-a)|Bj><Bj|, preserving entropy f(a).

Assign each such orbit total weight r_j/(1-a), and Bi weight (r_i-a)/(1-a). These weights are nonnegative, sum to one and reproduce r. Their average entropy is [(1-r_i)/(1-a)]f(a)+[(r_i-a)/(1-a)]L=U(r_i). This ensemble has at most nineteen entries and attains the lower bound, including terminal boundaries and vertices.
