join us

Open positions, application timelines, and how to get in touch.

We have funded openings for 2027: two postdocs and at least three PhD students, in Computer Science and in Physics.

I’m starting the group at NUS in December 2026, with a joint appointment in the School of Computing and the Department of Physics. We work on quantum advantage and where classical simulation runs out, on simulating many-body and open quantum systems, and on machine learning for quantum computers. The research page has the details. There’s room for theory, numerics and hardware-facing projects, for working with industry partners including Quantinuum, Google and IBM, and for collaborating with groups across North America, Europe and Asia.

You don’t need a quantum background. Physics, computer science and applied maths are all good starting points, and a lot of what I care about comes down to algorithms, optimisation or statistics. If you’re not sure you fit, write anyway.

Postdocs

Apply through Academic Jobs Online by 15 November 2026. Positions start in 2027, or from December 2026 if you’re ready earlier.

If you have your own fellowship, or want to apply for one, I’m happy to host you. These are the ones that fit:

  • Schmidt AI in Science Postdoctoral Fellowship: NUS is one of nine host universities. Up to two years.
  • NUS Fellows Programme (Southeast Asia): six to twelve months, for researchers from ASEAN countries. We write the proposal together.
  • NRF Fellowship: for people within about seven years of their PhD who want to run their own programme.
  • CQT Alice Fellowship: for women researchers in quantum science, based at CQT.
  • Fellowships from your home country, such as MSCA, SNSF, NSERC, JSPS or Humboldt. Get in touch early and we’ll work on the proposal together.

PhD students

Apply to the NUS PhD programme in Computer Science or in Physics. If you’re admitted you’re funded: the NUS Research Scholarship covers tuition and a monthly stipend, and you ask for it on the same application form.

The two departments have different deadlines:

Intake Physics Computer Science SINGA
August 2027 15 November 2026 15 December 2026 1 December 2026
January 2028 15 May 2027 15 June 2027 1 June 2027

SINGA is a separate scholarship for international students, with its own application. Scholarships are given out as good applications come in, so apply early rather than on the deadline.

You may not be able to pick me as a supervisor on the form yet, because I don’t start until December. Name me in your research statement and email me, so I know to look for your application.

Two other routes. If you’re a Singapore Citizen or PR, the Industrial PhD Programme lets a company and EDB fund your PhD here; the project is set up jointly, so talk to me first. And the National Quantum Scholarships Scheme funds quantum PhDs. It needs a CQT supervisor, and I’m happy to co-supervise with a CQT colleague.

Visitors

Long visits work best, ideally six to twelve months and at least three. Visitors usually bring their own funding, from a national scholarship council, a joint-PhD or exchange programme, or their home institution. If you’re applying for one of these, I’ll write the invitation letter and help with the application. Ask early, because the host letter is usually needed well before the scheme’s own deadline.

Master’s, FYP and UROP students: once I’m on campus.

Singapore

The National Quantum Processor Initiative is building trapped-ion and neutral-atom machines here, and Quantinuum opened an R&D centre in Singapore in March 2026. Singapore will be the first country outside the US to host its Helios machine.

Getting in touch

Email me at zhangyx@comp.nus.edu.sg with your CV and a paragraph on what you’d like to work on. A specific reaction to one of our papers tells me much more than general interest. If you’re applying for a PhD, say when and which scholarship. I read everything, though replies can be slow.
