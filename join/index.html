Open positions, application timelines, and how to get in touch.

I am starting a group at NUS in December 2026, jointly in the School of Computing and the Department of Physics, and I am recruiting at every level (see my NUS faculty profile).

We work on quantum advantage and the boundary of classical simulation, quantum simulation of many-body and open systems, and machine learning for quantum computing — see research for what that means in practice. Theory-leaning, numerics-leaning, and hardware-facing projects are all available, and qualified candidates will also have opportunities to work with industry partners in quantum computing, including Quantinuum, Google, and IBM, and to take part in an ongoing collaboration spanning North America, Europe, and Asia.

Open positions

  • Postdoctoral researchers — from December 2026. Funded positions; candidates holding or applying for their own fellowship are welcome too, and I am glad to host such applications.
  • PhD students — through the NUS graduate programmes in Computer Science or Physics. Funding is normally through an NUS Research Scholarship, SINGA, or the National Quantum Scholarships Scheme; see the deadlines below.
  • Visiting researchers and students — for focused projects, typically three months or longer.
  • Master's, FYP and UROP students — once I am on campus.
Backgrounds. Physics, computer science, and applied mathematics are all natural starting points. Prior quantum experience helps but is not required — several of the problems I care about are, at heart, questions about algorithms, optimisation, or statistics. If you are not sure whether you fit, write anyway.

Singapore

Singapore is expanding hard in quantum, on both the academic and the industrial side. The National Quantum Strategy committed about S$300 million over five years in 2024, and quantum was named a pillar of the wider RIE2030 research plan in 2026. Academically that is building out the Centre for Quantum Technologies at NUS into a national R&D centre with nodes across the local institutions, alongside A*STAR’s Quantum Innovation Centre; the National Quantum Processor Initiative is developing trapped-ion and neutral-atom hardware — the platforms closest to my own experimental work — and the National Quantum Scholarships Scheme funds up to 100 PhD and 100 master’s students over five years.

Industry has grown alongside it. Quantinuum has established an R&D centre here and a national partnership with the Quantum Office; the National Quantum-Safe Network is moving from testbed to nationwide deployment; and CQT spin-offs such as SpeQtral and Horizon Quantum Computing are based in the city. For a group working on verifiable advantage and hardware-facing simulation, having the machines and the people who build them within a few kilometres of the office matters more than it sounds.

The city itself is compact and English-speaking, a short flight from most of Asia while staying well connected to Europe and North America — which makes collaborations across three continents a good deal easier to sustain than they sound.

Funding

For 2027 the group has two postdoctoral openings and at least three PhD openings, all funded. You may also want to consider the external awards below; you are welcome to contact me about applying together.

For PhD applicants

  • NUS Research Scholarship — the default route; awarded through the graduate programme you apply to.
  • SINGA — for international applicants in the physical sciences and engineering, which covers this group's work. Deadlines below.
  • National Quantum Scholarships Scheme — quantum-specific, funding up to 100 PhD and 100 master's students over five years under the National Quantum Strategy.
  • A*STAR Graduate Scholarship — for Singapore citizens and permanent residents.
  • President's Graduate Fellowship — for exceptional candidates, open to all nationalities. You are nominated by the University rather than applying directly, so it is not something to plan around — but worth knowing it exists.
  • CQT graduate programme — a quantum-specific route into NUS, worth looking at alongside the CS and Physics programmes.

For postdoctoral applicants

  • NRF Fellowship — for researchers within about seven years of the PhD who are ready to run an independent programme; it funds independence rather than a mentored postdoc, and needs a host institution to put you forward.
  • CQT Alice Postdoctoral Fellowship — supports women researchers in quantum science and technology at CQT.
  • NUS postdoctoral fellowships — the University-wide listing; schemes here change, so check it directly.
  • Portable fellowships from your own region — MSCA, SNSF, NSERC, JSPS, Humboldt and similar schemes can often be held abroad. If you are applying for one, write early — I am glad to act as host and to work on the proposal with you.

Schemes, stipends and deadlines change from year to year, so treat the links above as the authoritative source and this list as a starting point — the full index of NUS graduate awards is here. If you are unsure which route fits, ask me — that is a much faster conversation than reading five policy pages.

Getting in touch

Email me at quantum.yxz@gmail.com with:

  • a CV,
  • a short note on what you would like to work on and why — a paragraph is plenty, and a specific reaction to one of the papers on the research page tells me far more than a general statement of interest,
  • for PhD applicants, when you intend to apply and which scholarship you are targeting.

I read everything that arrives, though replies can be slow during heavy stretches.
